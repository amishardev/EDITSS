# MOONLIGHT · AMISHAR · Marketing plan

Progressive house, 128 BPM, C minor. 21 Reels, one visual world, built entirely in Remotion.

> **One line:** you don't know exactly where you're going, but you're moving anyway.

## 1. Song analysis

Full numbers in `analysis/SONG_ANALYSIS.md` and `analysis/moonlight.analysis.json`. The short version:

- **Tempo 128.00 BPM.** The MP3 tag says 130. It's wrong: a kick phase-fit on both drops lands on 127.97-128.00, and the file is exactly 121 bars of 1.875 s. Every edit in this repo is on the 128 grid that starts at 0.000 s.
- **Harmony:** one loop the whole way through, **Ab - Fm - Cm - Bb**. It never resolves to a bright chord, it just keeps turning. That is the song's meaning in harmonic form: moving without landing.
- **Arrangement:**

| Time | Section | What matters for the edit |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:15 | Intro | keys only |
| 0:15.96 | first vocal | "Moving my feet to the beat of a drum" |
| 0:30.00 | kick + sub in | lands right under "alive" |
| 0:33.3 | "moonlight" | followed by a 1.4 s breath |
| 0:45-1:00 | build 1 | claps every beat, **kick drops out at 0:50.63** (9.4 s with no floor), 8th roll 0:52.5, 16th roll 0:56.25 |
| **1:00.000** | **drop 1** | lands on "grooving till the bar kicks us **OUT**" |
| 1:45.00 | breakdown | the last kick, then vocal + keys: "grooving till the bar kicks us out / oh, oh" |
| 2:00.00 | verse 2 | kick back, no claps; **"Shoot for the stars... We rise and we fall, living forever young"** (sung once in the whole song), 2.2 s silence between the two lines |
| 2:30-2:45 | build 2 | same shape as build 1 |
| **2:45.000** | **drop 2** | strongest section. **Kick drops out for bar 104 (3:13.1-3:15.0) and slams back at 3:15.000**, drops out again for bar 112 |
| 3:30-3:47 | outro | last hit at 3:30.000, fade |

- **Drums and bass in the drops:** kick on every beat, sub note on the 16th straight after each kick (sidechained rolling bass), pads pumping, offbeat open hat, claps on 2 and 4. The Cm bar (bar 3 of every 4-bar phrase) carries about 3 dB more bass than the rest, so there's a heavier "landing" every 7.5 s.
- **Strongest hook:** 0:56-1:01, the line that announces its own drop.
- **Strongest dance section:** drop 2, especially the kick-out and slam at 3:13-3:16.
- **Emotional peak:** 2:00-2:14, the bridge lyric.
- **Intimate moments:** intro, breakdown 1:45-1:59, the 2-second gap at 2:05.7.
- **Replayable moments:** 1:00.000, 3:15.000, "we rise" at 2:07.99, the kick entry at 0:30.00.

How the music drives the picture (used in every Reel):

| Musical element | Visual behaviour |
|---|---|
| Kick | streetlamp/pole/mast passes overhead, 0.6-1.5% scale punch, puddle ring |
| Clap (2 & 4) | where cuts are allowed in a groove edit, 3 px vertical accent |
| Bass (heavy Cm bar) | slow deep push, the "big image" of each phrase, arms up behind the glass |
| Build rolls | lamps/fixtures double to 8ths then 16ths, matte closes, zoom creeps in |
| Kick-out | float: punches stop, the frame narrows, things go still |
| Vocal | lyric type, word by word on the sung timing; lyric glows slightly with the vocal level |
| Drop / impact | the single biggest change in the Reel: a cut, an exit, a flash, a flip, a shatter |
| Pause / breath | hold, freeze, or a single quiet event (the shooting star in the 2:05 gap) |

## 2. Emotional identity

C minor depth, but the record is not sad. It's the feeling of being out late with your people, slightly aware it won't last, choosing to keep going anyway.

Five words: **forward, late, honest, warm, bittersweet.**

The emotional contrast the campaign holds onto:

- C minor depth → cool moonlight, honesty, the truth lines
- forward movement → lamps, roads, tunnels, trains, everything passing
- youthful freedom → running shadows, rooftops, the open road
- late-night energy → bars, neon, glass full of dancers, 2am
- bittersweet → the bar closing, lights going out, dawn arriving

## 3. Marketing story

**Everything moves except the moon.**

The city streams past all night: streetlamps, train windows, tunnels, other people's windows, the bar. The moon stays where it is, the one fixed point, the place where you say the true thing ("I promise to say it in the moonlight"). The night runs from leaving home to being kicked out at 2am to the long way home at sunrise.

Narrative arc across the campaign: **MOVEMENT → ESCAPE → RISK → TRUTH → NIGHT → FREEDOM → YOUTH → RISE → AFTERGLOW.**

Recognition target: by the end, a viewer connects **AMISHAR + "moonlight" + sodium lamps passing on the kick + the moon that never moves** without needing the caption.

## 4. Visual language

No stock footage, no live-action shoot, no AI images. Everything is procedural: canvas-drawn light, perspective, haze and grain, rendered frame by frame against the audio.

- **People only as shadows, silhouettes on a ledge, or blurred shapes behind steamed glass.** No faces, no models, no CGI humans. The environment does the acting.
- **Light is the subject.** Sodium lamps, tunnel strips, neon tubes, windows, the moon. Lights pass on the kick, so motion and rhythm are the same thing.
- **Palette:** ink navy (never pure black) · sodium orange `#ffa04a` · warm white `#ffe6c4` · moon blue `#c9dcf2` · rose neon `#ff5a7a` · dawn peach/cyan for the afterglow. Warm = people and places. Cool = the moon, truth, distance.
- **Texture:** fine film grain on every frame, soft bloom/halation on light sources, vignette, a tiny gate weave so nothing is locked-off digital.
- **Camera:** slow push-ins, gentle handheld drift, cinematic mattes that close during builds and snap open on drops.
- **Type:**
  - Hooks: Archivo, lowercase, small, upper third. A thought, not an ad.
  - Lyrics: Instrument Serif italic, word by word on the measured vocal timing.
  - Environmental type: the lyric is painted on the road, written on tunnel panels, glowing as a neon sign. The world says the words.
  - Labels: JetBrains Mono caps for times and places (11:58 PM, 4:52 AM, 0:50.63 kick out).
  - Title lockup: wide-tracked AMISHAR + serif "moonlight". Used twice in the whole campaign.

**Why this order**

- **A, curiosity (1-4).** No title, no drop. They establish the world (sodium, moon, shadows), the central idea (moving anyway) and the two risk images (dice, out of sight). Two of them stop right before a drop on purpose. The only branding is the account itself.
- **B, recognition (5-8).** The voice and the lines: "it's not where you been", "only fools lie", the breakdown, "we rise and we fall". People should start recognising the vocal before they've heard a drop.
- **C, song identity (9-11).** The hook line and drop 1 arrive, always tied to a story (the words on the road, 2am, the bar closing). The title and pre-save go into captions here.
- **D, strongest moments (12-16).** Drop bodies and drop 2, five different reactive approaches, daily, building to release.
- **E, release push (17-19).** The title lights up on the drop on release day; a credibility piece for producers/DJs; the full emotional arc in the long cinematic one.
- **F, long-tail (20-21).** Built for rewatching and discovery: a perfect loop and a reverse-narrative piece that rewards people who saw the rest.

After D+12: repost the two best performers with a new hook, and build new edits from the library (see README, "Making a new Reel").

## 8. Hook strategy

- First second always has a reason to stop: an image already in motion plus a short line that sounds like a thought.
- Hooks are lowercase, under ~8 words, never mention the song, the artist or a release.
- Three types, rotated so no two neighbouring posts use the same one:
  - **Recognition** ("the part before the night starts.", "walking home slower than we need to.", "when the lights come on.")
  - **Curiosity** ("the second roll is the one you remember.", "nothing moved until it did.", "you'll know it when the sign comes on.")
  - **Observation** ("the moon still follows the car.", "even the windows know this one.", "watch the lamps.")
- The best musical moment arrives fast: 12 of 21 Reels reach their payoff inside 8 seconds; the slow ones (1, 8, 19) are slow on purpose and say so in the first frame.
- A/B option: every Reel can be re-rendered with a different hook by editing one line in its spec.

All 21 hooks: the part before the night starts · the moon still follows the car · the second roll is the one you remember · out of sight. not out of mind · no plan was the plan · everything sounds more honest after midnight · walking home slower than we need to · for the ones who stayed out with you · the signs were on the road the whole time · 1:59. nobody's going home · when the lights come on · even the windows know this one · you could see the party from the street · the exit is the best part · nothing moved until it did · the summer nobody slept · you'll know it when the sign comes on · 9.4 seconds with no kick. that's the whole trick · we took the long way home. on purpose · watch the lamps · start at the end.

## 9. Caption strategy

- First line continues the hook's thought instead of repeating it (e.g. hook "when the lights come on." / caption "the bar kicked us out. the night didn't.").
- Phase A-B: caption is only the line. No hashtags wall, no "new music".
- Phase C onward: second line names the song once, plainly: `moonlight - AMISHAR`.
- Release week: second line `moonlight - out everywhere`, link lives in bio / pinned comment, not in the caption body.
- 3-5 hashtags maximum, genre and mood, the same set every time so the audio page and hashtag page reinforce each other: `#progressivehouse #melodichouse #latenight #AMISHAR`.
- Always upload the original audio and name it **AMISHAR - Moonlight**. Every Reel feeds the same audio page, and that page becomes the discovery engine.
- Pin a comment with the timestamp of the moment ("1:00.000 is the reason this exists") on the drop Reels.

## 10. CTA strategy

- **Never in the first 3 seconds, never as the hook.**
- On-screen CTA appears in exactly two Reels: the title lockup in 17 and 19. Everything else lets the caption and the audio page do the work.
- Pre-release (from post 9): soft "pre-save in bio" as the last caption line.
- Release day to D+7: "moonlight is out. link in bio." in captions only, plus a story linking to each Reel.
- Long-tail: CTA is "use this sound": invite people to post their own night with the audio, and reshare the best ones to stories.

## 11. Visual rules

1. Every cut lands on a measured musical event (bar line, clap, drop, kick-out, vocal entry). No cut "because it felt long".
2. The biggest visual change in a Reel is reserved for its biggest musical moment.
3. React to the phrase, not the metronome: kick punches stay at 0.5-1.5%; the heavy bass bar gets the deep push; kick-out bars float.
4. Lights are rhythm. When a light passes, it passes on the grid.
5. People are shadows, silhouettes or blur. Never faces.
6. Ink, sodium and moon blue are the base palette in every Reel. Dawn colours only after 3:30 or in the afterglow Reels.
7. Type lives in the safe area (top 13% to 80%), never under Instagram's UI.
8. Lyrics appear on the sung timing or not at all.
9. Grain, bloom and vignette on every frame: nothing ships "clean digital".
10. Loops end on a bar line of the Ab-Fm-Cm-Bb loop so the audio wraps musically.

## 12. Things to avoid

- Stock footage of any kind, especially the usual EDM set: sports cars, champagne, DJ hands, crowd hands, models dancing to camera, beach drones, generic sunsets, fireworks, mountain drones, neon cyberpunk streets, motivational business, staring-out-the-window shots.
- AI-generated landscapes or faces.
- Strobing, RGB splits, random glitch, shaking the whole frame, a flash on every beat, constant zoom pulses, audio-visualiser bars.
- "NEW SONG OUT NOW", "STREAM NOW", "LINK IN BIO" as hooks; "this goes crazy"; "you need to hear this".
- Spending the drop in every post. Drop 1's entry appears in 3 Reels, drop 2's entry in 2.
- Literal lyric illustration for every line (the dice and the painted road are the only literal ones, and both are used for the hook lines that earn it).
- Making it sad. C minor gives depth; the campaign stays warm, forward and a little reckless.
